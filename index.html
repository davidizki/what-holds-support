01 / The shift

Saving is not learning.

Your bookmarks remember where something was. What Holds helps you remember why it mattered—and whether it still holds up.

Before

A river of tabs, highlights, and forgotten good intentions.

Collection feels productive in the moment. Retrieval is the real test later.

After

A living body of references, capabilities, and claims with different jobs.

Every source has a purpose. Reference material stays findable; learned material gets practice; changing claims return for new evidence.

The compounding effect

Knowledge changes from something you own into something you can call on.

Not a second brain that thinks for you. A practice that makes your first one stronger.

02 / Built on learning science

A product system, not a productivity superstition.

What Holds separates robust findings from product inference. Retrieval, spacing, generation, feedback, and transfer inform the practice. The exact schedule is a transparent heuristic, and the separate evidence clock is an epistemic safeguard—not a claim that one algorithm has solved human learning.

Generation before automation

Produce something before the app produces something for you.

Generating an answer can improve later memory compared with only reading one, although the benefit depends on task and material. What Holds therefore makes the pre-processing prediction optional, brief, and safe to get wrong.

Bertsch et al., 2007 meta-analysis ↗

Effortful recall

Remembering is not only an outcome. The attempt is part of the learning.

What Holds asks for an answer before reveal. Retrieval-practice studies and meta-analyses generally find better delayed retention than restudy, while also showing that task design and feedback matter.

Rowland, 2014 meta-analysis ↗

Distributed encounters

The useful gap depends partly on how long the knowledge needs to last.

What Holds delays the first review, then widens successful gaps. The chosen horizon changes them only modestly because spacing research supports the principle—not one universally optimal interval for every learner and goal.

Cepeda et al., 2008 ↗

Practise future use

Being able to restate an idea is not the same as being able to use it.

Retrieval can transfer beyond the exact practised question, but transfer is variable and falls as the new task becomes less similar. “Use it” therefore asks for the real future situation and rehearses application, not only definition recall.

Pan & Rickard, 2018 meta-analysis ↗

Confidence before feedback

A confident error is not just another “hard” answer.

Recording confidence before reveal exposes calibration instead of hindsight. High-confidence errors can be especially correctable when clear feedback arrives, so a confident miss in What Holds pauses for a self-generated correction.

Metcalfe & Finn, 2011 ↗

Product inference: memory ≠ truth

A claim can be memorable and obsolete at the same time.

What Holds maintains two queues: memory practice based on recall, and evidence renewal based on how quickly a claim may change. The 30-, 90-, and 365-day defaults are visible heuristics; rerunning research replaces the old result in place.

Inspect the evidence boundary ↓

What the evidence does not say: learning outcomes depend on prior knowledge, material, task, feedback, context, and timing. The cited findings support design directions, not a universal memory multiplier, a guaranteed transfer effect, or proof that these scheduling constants are optimal for you.

03 / The practice

From passing information to durable capability.

One clear loop, repeated whenever a source deserves a place in your life.

  1. 01↘

    Capture the source

    Bring in the actual article, transcript, document, audio, or text—not a title pretending to be content.

  2. 02◇

    Choose its treatment

    Concept, Summary, Fact-check, Analysis, or exact Copy decides the shape of the result.

  3. 03✓

    Name the future use

    Find later, Understand, Recall, Use it, or Keep current decides whether this needs memory, transfer, or new evidence.

  4. 04↺

    Try before automation

    When you mean to learn, write one quick prediction before the app gives you a polished result.

  5. 05↗

    Retrieve and correct

    Attempt first, record confidence, reveal the grounded answer, and explain a confident mismatch in your own words.

  6. 06↗

    Transfer or refresh

    Practise the situation you named—or rerun evidence when a changing claim becomes due.

06 / Trust is part of the interface

Clear enough for serious work.

Your library stays local. When Apple Intelligence is available, it handles every intelligent stage on device—even long-source synthesis and Fact-check reasoning. Its sessions have a finite context, so supported long sources are divided into complete ordered units and validated across multiple on-device sessions instead of being quietly truncated. If it cannot finish reliably, nothing is silently downgraded or saved: you decide whether to retry or use Standard processing. Every note says how it was made.
