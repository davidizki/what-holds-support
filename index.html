Source first, or stop

The app works from what the source actually says.

A title, video footer, or set of page controls is not enough. What Holds first gets the actual article, transcript, or text, then keeps that exact material beside the note it creates.

Articles and public captions

What Holds looks for the article rather than menus and footers. If a public page loads its text later, the app can briefly open that page without keeping its cookies. YouTube links use available public captions—not the video or audio—and stop clearly when no usable captions are available.

Podcasts and audio

Publisher transcripts and podcast-feed transcripts come first. When a publisher exposes audio, supported system-26 devices can transcribe it on device; the temporary download is removed afterwards.

See exactly what was used

Every note shows where its text came from, how many words were read, how the note was made, the complete stored text, and the original link when one exists.

Intelligence without setup

No model menus. No cloud account.

What Holds uses the best private option available on your device and tells you, in plain language, how each note was made.

  1. Apple on-device intelligence When Apple Intelligence is available, What Holds uses it on your device to understand the source and build the note. For Fact-check, it cleans noisy source wording, keeps the raw fragment visible, and reasons over evidence found by the app.
  2. Built-in on-device processing If Apple Intelligence is unavailable, the source is too long, or the model cannot finish reliably, What Holds still creates a useful note on your device.
  3. Links before confident answers Automatic Fact-check retrieves relevant general-reference, cross-domain scholarly, and biomedical evidence before judging a claim. Optional OpenAI research can search the wider live web. In both cases, the links are the evidence—not simply what a model says.
Availability varies. The app itself supports iOS/iPadOS 17 or later and macOS 14 or later. Apple Intelligence also needs a compatible device, language, and region, and must be ready on iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26 or later. The built-in option works without it.

Advanced users: you can choose an OpenAI-compatible model service instead. If you do, the source title, link, and full text are sent to that service under its own terms. Remote services need HTTPS; a model running on localhost or a private local network may use HTTP. This is optional and does not connect to a ChatGPT subscription. Read the setup and privacy details.

Questions before buying

Straight answers.

Do I need Apple Intelligence?

No. Compatible devices get richer on-device processing automatically when Apple Intelligence is ready. Every supported device can still use the built-in private option.

Why doesn’t What Holds ask me to log in?

Because the core app does not need an account. Apple handles the purchase, and your library stays on your device. We do not need your email address, a password, or a cloud copy of your notes to make What Holds work.

Do I need an AI subscription or API key?

No. Apple-first Fact-check and standard processing work without either. Wider web research is optional and needs a separately billed OpenAI Platform API key; it cannot use or connect to a ChatGPT subscription. The advanced custom-model option is also optional.

Is Fact-check a professional verification service?

No. Automatic Fact-check asks Apple Intelligence to form readable claims grounded in the raw source and compare them with a small, relevance-filtered set of public reference and scholarly evidence. The app’s built-in scan takes over otherwise but does not infer truth from word overlap. Optional OpenAI research can search more current web sources and save clickable citations. Both are research aids: “Unsupported” means the selected method did not find enough supporting evidence, not that the claim is proven false.

Does the first version sync between devices?

No. Each device keeps its own local library. Use JSON export for a portable backup and import it deliberately on another device.
