01 / The shift

Saving is not learning.

Your bookmarks remember where something was. What Holds helps you remember why it mattered—and whether it still holds up.

Before

A river of tabs, highlights, and forgotten good intentions.

Saving feels productive. Later, you often cannot remember why the source mattered.

After

Useful notes you can find, trust, and act on.

Keep references easy to find, practise only what you want to learn, and recheck claims that may change.

What changes

You can explain the idea, use it, and check whether it is still true.

The app does not try to think for you. It helps you work with the knowledge you want to keep.

02 / Why it helps

Built on how people actually learn.

Research points to a simple pattern: we remember more when we try, recall, get feedback, return after a delay, and practise using an idea. What Holds turns those findings into clear habits—not promises of a perfect memory.

Try before you see

Make an attempt before the app gives you an answer.

Trying to produce an answer can improve later memory compared with only reading one. The benefit depends on the task and material, so What Holds keeps this prediction brief, optional, and safe to get wrong.

Bertsch et al., 2007 meta-analysis ↗

Recall before reveal

Trying to remember is part of learning.

What Holds asks you to answer before revealing the note. Studies generally find better delayed memory when people try to recall than when they simply reread, although the question and feedback still matter.

Rowland, 2014 meta-analysis ↗

Return after a gap

Reviews work better when they are spread out.

What Holds delays the first review, then widens the gap after successful recall. Research supports spacing, but not one perfect interval for every person, topic, and goal.

Cepeda et al., 2008 ↗

Practise the real use

Explaining an idea is not the same as using it.

Practice can help in a new situation, but the benefit drops when that situation is very different. “Use it” asks where you will need the idea and rehearses that situation.

Pan & Rickard, 2018 meta-analysis ↗

Confidence before feedback

Say how sure you are before seeing the answer.

Choosing confidence before reveal avoids hindsight. A confident mistake receives extra attention: What Holds pauses and asks you to explain the correction in your own words.

Metcalfe & Finn, 2011 ↗

Remembering is not verification

A claim can be memorable and out of date.

What Holds keeps two schedules: one based on how well you remember, and one based on how quickly the claim may change. The evidence dates are simple, visible defaults—not a scientific law.

See how evidence is handled ↓

The honest limit: results vary by person, topic, task, timing, and feedback. These studies guide the design; they do not promise perfect memory or prove that one review schedule is best for everyone.

03 / How it works

From saved to useful.

A simple loop for the sources that matter.

  1. 01↘

    Add the source

    Bring in the article, transcript, document, audio, or text itself—not just its title.

  2. 02◇

    Choose what you need

    Make one concept, a summary, a fact-check, an analysis, or an exact copy.

  3. 03✓

    Say why you are saving it

    Keep it to find, understand, remember, use, or recheck later.

  4. 04↺

    Try first

    If you want to learn it, write one quick thought before the app processes the source.

  5. 05↗

    Review without peeking

    Answer, choose your confidence, reveal the source-based answer, and correct mistakes in your own words.

  6. 06↗

    Use it or recheck it

    Practise the situation you named, or look for fresh evidence when a claim is due.

06 / How your data is handled

Private by default. Clear when anything goes online.

Your library stays on your device. When Apple Intelligence is available, it processes notes on device. Long sources are split into ordered sections so none are silently cut off. If processing fails, the app saves nothing and lets you retry or choose Standard processing. Every note shows how it was made.

See what leaves your device Get help →

Why long sources are split: Apple documents a 4,096-token limit for each on-device model session. What Holds leaves room for instructions and output, then processes supported larger sources in ordered sections. Read Apple’s technical note ↗
